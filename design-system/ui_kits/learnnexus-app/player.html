Lawful bases explained

Module 1 · Foundations Video · 12 min Updated 12 Jul 2026

Before you process anyone's personal data, you need a lawful basis. There are six of them, and picking the right one is not a formality — it decides which rights the person can exercise against you afterwards.

You must decide your lawful basis before processing begins, and document it. Switching later is only permitted in narrow circumstances.

The six bases

  • Consent — freely given, specific and withdrawable. Weakest in an employment context, because the power imbalance undermines "freely given".
  • Contract — processing necessary to deliver what the person asked for.
  • Legal obligation — a statute requires it of you.
  • Vital interests — someone's life is at stake. Rare.
  • Public task — official authority or a task in the public interest.
  • Legitimate interests — the most flexible, and the only one that requires you to balance your interest against the person's rights.

In practice, most internal HR processing runs on contract or legal obligation, and most product analytics runs on legitimate interests — with a documented balancing test on file.
